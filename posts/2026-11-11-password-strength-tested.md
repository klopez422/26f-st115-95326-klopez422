# Password Strength, Tested

## Introduction

Passwords are something that almost everyone uses every day. Whether we are logging into social media, checking our emails, or accessing our bank accounts, passwords help protect our personal information. Even though passwords are important, many people still use simple passwords that are easy to remember. The problem is that these passwords can also be easy for hackers to guess. My topic is password strength and how different passwords can affect someone's online security.

I chose this topic because cybersecurity is becoming more important as technology continues to grow. People store a lot of personal information online, and having a weak password can put that information at risk. For example, someone who uses the same password for multiple accounts could have several accounts exposed if one website experiences a data breach. Hackers can also use methods like brute-force attacks, where they try different password combinations until they find the correct one.

One important part of password security is understanding what actually makes a password strong. Some people think that adding a number or symbol automatically makes their password safe. However, a password can still be predictable even with those characters. Longer passwords that are unique and difficult to guess are usually better choices. Password managers can also help people create and store stronger passwords without having to memorize every single one.

Another part of this topic involves how websites protect passwords. Technologies like hashing and salting help prevent stored passwords from being easily exposed if someone gains access to a database. Hashing changes a password into a value that is not meant to be reversed, while salting adds random data during the hashing process. These methods are important because protecting passwords is not only the user's responsibility but also the responsibility of the websites storing them.

The main question I want to answer in this blog is: What makes a password strong, and how can different security methods help protect it from hackers? I plan to look at common password weaknesses, explain different types of attacks, and compare examples of weak and strong passwords. By the end of this project, I want readers to understand why password security matters and what they can do to better protect their online accounts.
